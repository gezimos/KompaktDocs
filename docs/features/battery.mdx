# Battery

Kompakt app → **Settings → Battery**.

## Charge limit

<ScreenshotRow>
  <Screenshot src="/img/screens/battery-kompakt.png" caption="Kompakt app → Settings → Battery: cycles, health, capacity, first used." />
  <Screenshot src="/img/screens/battery-kompakt-2.png" caption="Swipe up: Stop charging at, Right now and Resume a little lower." />
</ScreenshotRow>

- **Stop charging at**: 80, 85, 90, 95 or 100%. Charging stops at that level.
- **Resume a little lower**: on, charging starts again a little under the
  limit, so the phone is not topped up for every percent it uses.
- It only stops charging. A battery already above the limit stays there until
  it falls below it.
- A limit under 100 makes the battery last more years.

**Right now** shows what the charger is doing:

| Row says | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Stopped at limit | Plugged in, charging held |
| Charging | Plugged in, charging |
| Full | At 100% |
| Not plugged in | On battery |

## Cycles, capacity and health

- **Cycles**: full-charge equivalents, not the number of times you plugged in.
  Also in Android's Settings → Battery → Battery information.
- **Capacity**: what the battery holds now, against when it was new.
- **First used**: set once, on first boot.
- **Health** comes from a fixed table and does not change as the battery ages.
  The cycle count shows wear.
- Cycles and the first-use date are kept outside Android, so they survive
  reinstalling, and going back to MuditaOS.

Android's own battery screens work as usual: **Battery usage** shows the level
and screen time since the last full charge, and **Battery Manager** limits apps
you rarely use.
